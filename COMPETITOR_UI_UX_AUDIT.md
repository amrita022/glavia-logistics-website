# Ultratrans competitor UI/UX audit

## 1. Executive Summary

**Scope.** This audit covers the public Ultratrans website as inspected on 23 November 2026 (India). Glavia Logistics is the working name for the proposed frontend-only informational site, based on the project name. The design direction is provisional until Glavia confirms its brand assets, service catalogue, coverage, and operating claims.

Ultratrans gives buyers several effective ways to find a service and contact the team. It pairs recognisable transport imagery with eight dedicated service pages, routes visitors to a quote with the selected service preserved, and explains what happens after an enquiry. Its strongest patterns reduce uncertainty. Glavia can advance those patterns with earlier service comparison, role-based contact, and proof tied to each buying decision.

The clearest observed problems were mobile chat covering nearby content, the desktop Services disclosure closing before Tab reaches its shortcuts, inconsistent milestones in the sample tracking timeline, and key tasks sitting well below promotional introductions. The report distinguishes observed behaviour, design judgements, and checks that need further testing. Severity indicates user impact within the audited journey, independent of WCAG conformance.

## 2. Competitor Website Overview

- [Ultratrans.in](https://ultratrans.in/) redirected to [the www homepage](https://www.ultratrans.in/) and returned HTTP 200. Search also surfaced older page versions. The findings refer to the rendered www experience and its current navigation.
- Ultratrans presents itself as a Mumbai-based logistics provider serving India and international destinations. It offers air, ocean and road freight, customs clearance, warehousing, supply chain management, packaging, and cross-border courier. Its experience, support availability, and displayed credentials are company claims. [Home](https://www.ultratrans.in/) · [Services](https://www.ultratrans.in/services) · [About](https://www.ultratrans.in/about-us#certifications)
- **Method.** Reviewed rendered Edge pages and public DOM, captured desktop and mobile views, inspected responsive geometry, sampled keyboard and pointer interactions, and checked a service-prefilled enquiry without sending data. Resource Timing was used for request observations only. Four CSS widths were sampled on the principal routes. All eight service details were opened at desktop width, with Ocean also sampled at all four widths.
- **Limits.** Viewports were emulated in desktop Edge. Real mobile Safari and Android, touch and soft-keyboard behaviour, screen-reader speech, formal WCAG conformance, end-to-end form delivery, chat replies, external app handoffs, and field performance were outside the verified scope. The requested `competitor-experience-audit`, `uxui-audit`, and `ui-ux-pro-max` skills were unavailable in the project and user skill inventory. The design system below uses direct audit evidence and published standards.
- **Evidence references.** Screenshot references in the findings are capture identifiers paired with a route, viewport, and reproduction steps. The report uses those descriptions for durable evidence, with browser captures kept outside the project.

## 8. Performance Observations

- **Observed.** The desktop home snapshot exposed 13 script resource entries and 19 fetch resource entries in Resource Timing after a navigation. Several below-fold certification and service images were marked lazy. The Contact page had a map iframe. These counts are navigation-specific and affected by caching, prefetching, viewport and observation time. Transfer sizes from this sequence are unsuitable for a cold-cache page-weight claim.
- **Potential costs.** A continuously rendered globe, decorative animations, multiple font families, app shell scripts, and map/chat dependencies are candidates to profile on a low-end phone. The audit did not isolate main-thread work, battery impact, image savings, or the cost of opening each widget.
- **Glavia performance plan.** Deliver HTML with working links and readable content on initial render. Use one responsive hero image, explicit dimensions, and modern encodings where beneficial. Prioritise the actual LCP asset, lazy-load below-fold imagery, keep a directions link in place of automatic map loading, and limit hydration to useful interactions. Start with one self-hosted variable font, adding a second only when the typography requires it.
- **Launch targets.** Aim at the 75th percentile for LCP ≤2.5 s, INP ≤200 ms and CLS ≤0.1 once field data is available. Use three or more cold-cache lab runs on representative templates before launch, record environment and median results, and treat the lab and field figures as distinct. As provisional budgets for key landing pages, aim for ≤200 KiB compressed initial JavaScript and ≤250 KiB for the mobile LCP image. Revise against measured needs, keeping hero and third-party payloads visible. [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals)

## 10. Competitor Strengths Worth Learning From

- **Context survives the buying journey.** The Ocean detail's quote link prefills Ocean. Apply the same principle whenever a visitor asks about a specific service. [Ocean](https://www.ultratrans.in/services/ocean-freight) · [Prefilled quote](https://www.ultratrans.in/enquiry?service=ocean-freight)
- **The footer works as a second directory.** It provides eight service links plus company information, privacy, directions and direct contact. Give visitors a useful recovery route after reaching the bottom. [Home](https://www.ultratrans.in/)
- **The site explains the handoff.** Review, callback and quote give visitors a picture of what comes next. Use truthful expectations and only publish a response window the team has approved. [Quote](https://www.ultratrans.in/enquiry)
- **Credibility has a dedicated destination.** Credentials have explanations, staff are grouped by function and CSR actions are described concretely. Use scoped evidence and approved operating examples for Glavia. [About](https://www.ultratrans.in/about-us)
- **Menus help people interpret unfamiliar offerings.** Desktop descriptions add useful context and mobile shortcuts save a step for known tasks. The active state and breadcrumbs support orientation. [Services](https://www.ultratrans.in/services)
- **Empty states have a recovery path.** Careers explains the absence of vacancies and gives applicants a next step and a submission checklist. [Careers](https://www.ultratrans.in/careers)

## 11. Proposed Superior Design System

**Design philosophy.** Make Glavia feel like an accountable operations partner. Use a light editorial layout, warm neutral surfaces, deep pine and teal, real freight imagery, useful captions, and compact service labels. Use a limited set of components with consistent alignment. Brand distinctiveness should come from approved copy and photography that show how the company works.

| Token | Value | Application |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Primary ink | `#102A2A` | Headings, primary text, dark footer background when paired with light text |
| Secondary text | `#425959` | Explanations and supporting labels on light surfaces |
| Page background | `#F8F7F2` | Warm overall canvas |
| Surface | `#FFFFFF` | Cards, panels and navigation surfaces |
| Primary action | `#00665E` with `#FFFFFF` label | Quote/contact handoff and selected location cues with an accompanying text or icon |
| Action hover | `#004F49` | Same label treatment, with a visible state transition |
| Brand accent | `#E7AE57` with `#102A2A` | Small data or editorial highlights |
| Subtle divider | `#D7E1DC` | Decorative separators. Use a darker border for controls that need visible boundaries |
| Control border | `#667E79` | Form fields and interactive boundaries on light backgrounds |
| Error | `#9B2525` | Inline error text and icon with a plain-language explanation |
| Focus | `#004F49` on light, `#F7C777` on dark | 2–3 px outline with offset. Add a contrasting separator ring when needed |

Calculated solid pairings include white on primary teal at 6.86:1, secondary text on the warm background at 6.98:1, and pine on the gold accent at 7.64:1. Check all realised pairings, alpha layers, and interactive states in the built site. Start with Instrument Sans for display and body, with a system sans fallback. Use 44–64 px desktop / 32–40 px mobile for H1, 28–40 px for H2, 20–24 px for H3, 16–18 px for body and 14–16 px for supporting text. Use body line-height 1.5–1.65 and heading line-height 1.1–1.25. Keep reading columns around 55–70 characters.

Use an 8 px base with 4 px substeps for alignment in small controls. Aim for 12 or 16 px gaps within related content, 20 or 24 px card padding, and 48–64 px between mobile sections or 64–96 px on wide screens. Use a 1200–1280 px content maximum, 16–24 px phone gutters and fluid wider gutters. Use 12–16 px card radii and restrained shadows. Specify default, hover, focus-visible, active, and disabled states where applicable. Keep text links identifiable without colour alone.

| Layout rule | Narrow | Medium | Wide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Starting viewport range | 320–599 px | 600–1023 px | 1024 px and above |
| Header | Wordmark and menu, with direct CTA when it fits | Drawer until navigation fits with long labels and zoom | Direct links, Services disclosure, one primary CTA |
| Services | One column with service name, short fit statement, and link | Two columns | Three columns where card content remains legible |
| Hero | Copy and actions, then image | Stack or split based on real content | Split with a restrained limiting height |
| Detail and contact | Single reading column | Two columns only where labels and links wrap neatly | 8/4 main content and complementary rail; avoid sticky rail when it covers content |

Breakpoints are starting values. Adjust at the widths where actual content stops fitting. Images use responsive source sizes and meaningful crops. Keep buttons wrapping and preserve focus rings near edges.

Label the external action clearly, for example “Email sales about Ocean Freight.” | Works within a frontend-only site and gives visitors a clear fallback when a mail app is absent. | Users see a copyable address, can change context, and see only genuine feedback such as “Copied”. Native copy errors leave text selectable. A future managed form requires separately approved delivery and privacy handling. |
| Responsive and interaction states | Keep mobile reading order aligned with desktop, allow cards to reflow and wrap long labels, and keep the screen edges available for navigation and focus. Use 120–180 ms colour/opacity transitions for feedback and support reduced motion. | Improves usability across phones, tablets, zoom and keyboard use. | Test 320/390/768/1024/1440, short landscape, zoom, text-spacing overrides, reduced motion and real-device touch. Reflow has no unintended overlap. |
| Accessible content and performance | Use native controls and landmarks, a skip link, descriptive links, consistent focus, image alternatives and readable status labels. Use optimised responsive media and limited client-side work. | Maintains clarity when users navigate by keyboard or assistive tech and when networks are slow. | Verify WCAG 2.2 AA on critical journeys with manual tests and an automated scan. Check Core Web Vitals and load budgets from Section 8. [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/) |

## 15. Final Recommendations

1. Put service selection and human contact in the first screen on core routes. Use page-specific introductions and preserve context through the contact handoff.
2. Establish a distinct light, pine-and-teal identity with authorised photography and a concise, content-led hero.
3. Make information useful during purchase decisions. Publish service scope, inputs, examples, and ownership with verified wording.
4. Treat WCAG 2.2 AA, narrow-screen layout, and performance budgets as acceptance criteria for the actual contact and discovery journeys.
5. Add the mode comparison before richer interactive aids. Expand the experience only where it answers a real shipping question or reduces effort.
